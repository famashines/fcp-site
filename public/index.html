FCP is an open-source front-camera perception and ADAS stack. It uses only open models, public datasets and published methods, and it runs on real recordings today.

What the camera finds

FCP shows only what applies to your lane: the vehicle you follow, the light you must obey, the sign that is for you.

  • Lanes

    Lane detection out to 60–80 m on the motorway. A second “far view” pass over the 120° fisheye image adds the long range.

    60–80 m

  • Vehicles and CIPV

    3D vehicle detection, selection of the closest in-path vehicle (CIPV) and time to collision (TTC). Filters remove oncoming traffic and false detections on guard rails.

    CIPV, TTC

  • Traffic light of interest

    Only the light that applies to your lane, with its state. The other lights at the junction stay out of the way.

    Your lane only

  • Traffic signs of interest

    Speed limits, no overtaking, priority road and more, shown only when they apply to you.

    When it applies

Driving functions, open loop

Three functions run on top of the perception output. They compute what the car should do, but they do not actuate it.

ACC
Adaptive cruise control. Keeps a set speed and a safe gap to the CIPV.
LKA
Lane keeping assist. Steers back toward the lane centre from the detected lanes.
FCW
Forward collision warning. Warns when the time to collision with the CIPV gets too short.

FCP Studio

A viewer for MCAP recordings, made for FCP. Camera view, 3D scene, bird’s-eye view, timeline and plots in one window, so you can see what the stack saw and why it decided.

Inside the stack

  1. Fisheye camera

    One 120° front camera. Calibration comes from the fisheye camera model.

  2. Multi-task network

    One network with a DINOv3 ViT-S backbone, trained on public datasets and running on TensorRT.

  3. Tracking and distance

    A camera-only pipeline with tracking and distance fusion.

  4. Driving functions

    ACC, LKA and FCW on top of the perception output, open loop.

  5. Recording and review

    Everything goes to MCAP and opens in FCP Studio.

How it is kept honest

  • A modular architecture.
  • A fault-injection suite that tests robustness.
  • A benchmark suite.
  • An issue tracker that links every issue to its fix.
